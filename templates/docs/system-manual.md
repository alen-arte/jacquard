> Design decisions live in `decisions.md` (area: design). This manual cites them by `D#`.

## §1 · The product's world
The concept that answers design questions before they come up. It names what the product already is; it is not decoration added on top. Why this world, for these people.

## §2 · Rules that decide for us
Five to seven principles. Each one: a title, the rule, and **"So that…"** (what it protects). When a question comes up, these settle it before the taste of the moment does.

## §3 · Shape grammar
The few families of elements and the shape of each (what speaks to you, what you touch, what measures or informs). The user learns the rule without being told.

## §4 · Colour
Each colour has one job. Tokens with names and values. What carries meaning, what is neutral, what is the user's own. Never colour alone.

## §5 · Type
Typographic voices, a closed scale (sizes and their uses), and the rules for numbers and data.

## §6 · The system applied
One real screen built with the system, and the list of what it decides.

## §7 · Light, motion and sound
Speeds with a meaning (response, feedback, ceremony, silence), budgets for light and motion, sound direction, reduced motion.

## §8 · Navigation and layers
Where everything lives (one home per thing), how deep anything can be, navigation per the user flows (`UF#`).

## §9 · Domain loops
The mechanics specific to this product's domain (progression, rewards, rhythms of use, alerts), with their purpose and their limits.

## §10 · Voice
How the product speaks: registers, when it speaks and when it stays quiet, examples of good and bad copy.

## §11 · Context of use
Device, distance, attention, environment, session length, and what each one demands from the design.

## §12 · Decisions and next steps
The design decisions (`D#`) that shape this manual, with their status, and what comes next.
